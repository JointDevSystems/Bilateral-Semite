Weighbridge

Every unit weighed, recorded and released on the right numbers.

Our on-site weighbridge lets us confirm the real weight of a loaded truck and container before it enters the yard or goes back on the road. Declared weights are checked against actual weights, so a mismatch is found at our gate and not at a roadside weighing station.

Each weighing produces a ticket tied to the truck, the container number and the booking. That record stays with the unit for as long as it is in our custody, and we can share it with you on request.

  • Gross, tare and net weight captured for every weighing
  • Declared weight compared with actual weight at gate-in
  • Weighing ticket linked to container, truck and booking
  • Re-weigh at gate-out when the load has changed

What each weighing records

  • Truck registration and driver details
  • Container number and seal number
  • Gross weight of truck and container together
  • Tare weight of truck and empty container
  • Net cargo weight, worked out from the two readings
  • Date, time and the operator who recorded it
Why weighing matters

Accurate weights protect your cargo, your schedule and your licence to operate.

Documentation

Shipping lines and terminals expect an accurate verified gross mass (VGM) for export containers. A weighbridge ticket from a loaded unit gives you a measured figure to support that declaration, and it helps catch errors before they reach the vessel.

Where a weight does not match the paperwork, we tell you before the unit moves on.

Road compliance

Overloaded trucks risk fines, offloading and long delays at public weighbridges along the corridor, and they damage the road. Checking the weight at our gate lets the load be corrected where it is cheapest and quickest to do so.

This matters most for heavy, dense and project cargo.

Safe handling

Our equipment operators plan lifts, stacks and trailer loading using real weights. Heavier units are placed and stacked accordingly, which makes yard work safer and protects the cargo inside.

Reliable weight data also supports clear charge and claim discussions.

How a weighing works

Four steps, a few minutes.

  1. ApproachThe truck is guided onto the weighbridge deck and the driver is checked in.
  2. WeighGross weight is read once the vehicle is stationary and settled on the deck.
  3. CompareThe reading is checked against the declared weight and the release or booking documents.
  4. RecordA ticket is issued and logged against the unit, and any variance is flagged to you.
